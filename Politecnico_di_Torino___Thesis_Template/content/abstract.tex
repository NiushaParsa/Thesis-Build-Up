Retrieval-augmented generation depends on finding useful evidence, but the
amount of text returned by a retriever is often fixed before a question is
seen. A short passage can isolate an important detail while losing its
context; a longer passage can preserve that context while introducing
irrelevant material. This thesis investigates whether retrieval granularity
can be adapted to a question and to the evidence distribution within its
source document.

The study uses QASPER scientific papers, five non-overlapping chunk sizes
from 10 to 160 GPT-2 tokens, fixed dense embeddings, and paper-restricted
top-five retrieval. The experimental sequence progresses from fixed-size
and mixed-size baselines to embedding-based routers, Qwen language-model
routers, hierarchical similarity features, leakage-controlled feature
fusion, expected-regret learning, and local tree-level routing. It also
examines unsuccessful training formulations and a constrained-output
repair for a zero-shot context feature.

On 924 validation questions, fixed 40-token retrieval achieves a mean
joined evidence-token F1 of 0.3159. An offline oracle that chooses the best
of the five fixed-size results for each question reaches 0.3821, indicating
potential value in adaptation within this action space. The strongest
learned question-level router instead reaches 0.3075: learning expected
retrieval regret improves on the preceding learned alternatives but does
not surpass the fixed baseline. Local tree-level routing reaches 0.3053,
compared with 0.3075 for a matched fixed-40 tree baseline. Adding a frozen
Qwen context-length feature produces no demonstrated improvement.

The main finding is that predicting an evidence-length category and
maximising retrieval usefulness are not equivalent. Objective alignment,
leakage control, and matched retrieval budgets matter more than model
complexity alone. These results concern lexical evidence retrieval, not
generated-answer quality, and repeated use of the validation split limits
claims about unseen-data generalisation.
